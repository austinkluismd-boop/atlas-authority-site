The Problem

How patients find you has changed. Permanently.

For two decades, a practice’s digital presence meant a website and a Google ranking. That world is gone. A prospective patient now moves through AI assistants that recommend specific practices by name, search pages that answer the question before a single click, map results, review platforms, and short-form video — and every one of those systems forms its own machine judgment about who you are.

Benchmarked 1 in 3 U.S. adults have used AI chatbots for health information and advice. KFF Health Information & Trust tracking poll
Benchmarked ~900M People using ChatGPT every week, worldwide. Company-reported, 2026
Benchmarked 15%→8% Users click a traditional result roughly half as often when an AI summary answers first. Pew Research Center, July 2025
Benchmarked Majority Of Google searches now end without a single click to any website. Zero-click analyses

“The underlying authority is not deficient.
Its digital representation is.”

Decades of training, thousands of cases, board certification, the respect of your peers — the authority is real. What search and AI systems reward is a complete, consistent, machine-readable corroboration of that authority, maintained continuously across every surface. They cannot reward what they cannot verify. Most practices do not have a visibility problem; they have incomplete entity corroboration — and no instrument that can even see it, let alone fix it and prove the fix worked.

Digital discovery behaves as layered infrastructure — technical foundation, entity corroboration, content depth, local presence, reputation, AI accessibility. The weakest layer binds the others. Atlas exists to find the constraint, fix it, and prove the fix moved the number.

The Discipline

Detect. Investigate. Adjudicate. Approve.

Automated rules detect. AI investigates why. A statistics engine decides whether it worked. A named human approves anything that touches your live presence. That division of labor is enforced by the code — not by a policy document.

The Intervention Ledger

Every change is written down before it happens — what, when, what it cost, and a pre-declared prediction of its effect. The record is append-only and tamper-evident: your practice’s digital history becomes an asset no one can rewrite.

The Causal Engine

When something changes, Atlas compares what happened against what the data says would have happened anyway — the interrupted time-series method family used in clinical-policy research. “Definitely caused” is not in its vocabulary; refusing to estimate is a feature.

Always-On Sentinels

Fourteen deterministic rules watch the panel daily — search clicks, map share, ratings, calls, site speed, AI-crawler access, uptime, SSL. Firings are logged, investigated, and dispositioned — not emailed into a void.

The Monthly Close

Business outcomes reconcile into a signed report with attribution confidence stated on every line — deterministic above documented, with the unattributed remainder reported, never allocated. Both parties sign.

An absence is recorded as an absence, never a silent zero. Atlas never fakes real-time.

By Construction

Patients never enter the system.

Two separate data planes

Marketing measurement lives in one store; business outcomes live in another. The boundary is structural — enforced by the architecture, not by a promise.

No patient identity

The import mapping has no field for names, birthdates, or addresses — they are never read. Phone and email become one-way salted codes at the instant of import.

No third-party tags

A permanent structural rule, not a preference. Analytics run self-hosted on infrastructure the practice controls; IP addresses are not stored.
